# Philatelic Valuation Research Notes

Philalens should estimate value through evidence-weighted ranges, not single
prices. The correct workflow is identification first, then variant and condition,
then market evidence.

## Valuation Factors

Important factors:

- exact stamp identity and catalog number
- country or issuing authority
- issue year and series
- denomination, color, and design variant
- perforation, watermark, paper, overprint, and printing variety
- used versus unused condition
- gum state for unused stamps
- centering and margins
- cancellation type, strength, date, and rarity
- faults such as tears, creases, stains, toning, clipped corners, thins, repairs,
  reperforation, or regumming
- rarity and supply
- collector demand and thematic appeal
- market venue and geography
- realized sale evidence versus asking prices

## Observable From A Front Album Photo

Usually observable:

- country or issuing authority
- denomination
- visible text
- design family
- dominant color
- cancellation presence and heaviness
- rough centering
- visible stains, tears, bends, missing corners, or surface damage

Sometimes observable:

- perforation completeness
- approximate perforation gauge
- subtle color differences
- postmark date/location

Usually not observable:

- watermark
- paper type
- gum condition
- hinge marks
- regumming
- thins on the reverse
- chemical alteration
- expertized authenticity

## Recommended Valuation Model

Use a confidence-band model:

1. Identify candidate stamp records.
2. Score identity confidence.
3. Attach catalog/reference value if legally available.
4. Retrieve market evidence.
5. Classify condition from visible evidence.
6. Apply condition and uncertainty adjustments.
7. Output a low/high range with evidence and assumptions.

## Evidence Tiers

Stronger evidence:

- realized auction/sale prices for the same catalog number and comparable
  condition
- expert certificate or authoritative expertizing result
- licensed catalog record
- user-provided catalog data with known source

Medium evidence:

- dealer prices for the same catalog number and condition
- active marketplace listings with clear matching photos
- multiple independent catalog/reference matches

Weak evidence:

- active asking prices without sold/completed evidence
- visually similar stamps without confirmed catalog number
- AI-only identity guesses

## Confidence Output

Every estimate should expose:

- identity confidence
- condition confidence
- market evidence confidence
- unobservable risk warnings
- recommended next action

Example next actions:

- "Likely common; no further review needed."
- "Needs better crop; denomination or country unclear."
- "Possible higher-value candidate; scan front and back."
- "Expert review recommended before relying on value."

## Practical Challenge

Many album-page collections contain large quantities of common used stamps with
low individual value. The product should optimize for triage: identify likely
common material quickly, surface possible outliers, and avoid spending too much
analysis cost on low-confidence low-value stamps.
